# Belmont Realty spec project — business plan

## The actual goal

Not a one-time redesign sale. The goal is for **Belmont Realty to hire me as their ongoing marketing person** — a part-time contractor role, not an invoice for a single deliverable. The redesign mockup (see `WEBSITE_PLAN.md`) is the audition piece that opens the conversation; a full-site-rebuild proposal is the pitch built on top of it once there's a relationship.

The other five realty companies on the original list (RHAMCO, BlueSky Living, The Arabella, NV Properties, Fordham Housing, LSANY) are **explicitly not part of this outreach plan.** They're a fallback to revisit only if Belmont doesn't work out — not a queue to run through in parallel, and not the reason this mockup was built.

## Who gets approached, and how

**Belmont Realty, alone, first.** Contact via the listed Gmail address (belmonthousing@gmail.com) with a link to the finished mockup, or a call to the listed number if that reads as more credible for a business that's been run by the same founder since 1984.

**The framing:** "I'm a Fordham student, I noticed some concrete gaps in how the site presents your properties, I built an example of what I'd change — and I'd like to help you fix this on an ongoing basis." Lead with the specific, provable gaps, not a generic "your site looks old":

- No pricing or availability shown anywhere on the site
- Reservation process is mail-in only ($600 deposit per person to a PO Box) — no online application or payment
- Property photos are roughly a decade old
- Genuinely good amenities (furnished, free maid service, utilities included) are buried in prose instead of sold

The pitch should describe the **role**, not just the mockup: ongoing site maintenance and updates, new photography as units turn over, listings and copy kept current, and — since I'm bilingual — Spanish-language materials as a stated future addition, given the neighborhood. Not built yet, but worth naming as part of what a marketing hire brings that the current setup doesn't have.

## What I charge, and why

**A low monthly retainer, offered as an explicit trial-period rate — not a one-time project fee.** A one-off invoice doesn't recur; a retainer does, and recurring revenue is the actual near-term goal ($100/month minimum), so the ask should be structured to produce that directly rather than requiring a string of one-off sales to add up to it.

**Anchor: $100-200/month to start**, explicitly framed as a trial (e.g., "let's try this for a semester") rather than a locked-in long-term rate. The full site rebuild — worth $1,500-3,000 as a standalone project in this market — gets offered as a larger one-time project *within* the retainer relationship once there's trust, not as the opening ask. Leading with the big number to someone with no track record invites a no; leading with a small, bounded trial doesn't.

## What to ask for besides money

- **A defined trial period with a specific end date** (e.g., end of the semester). This protects me from being stuck at an introductory rate indefinitely, and protects Belmont from committing long-term to an unproven contractor — it's a fair ask on both sides.
- **A written testimonial or reference** once there's real delivered work to point to, useful even if this specific engagement doesn't continue past the trial.
- **A clear, explicit scope agreement** for what $100-200/month actually covers. That rate does not fund a full backend rebuild — say so up front, so it doesn't quietly turn into unpaid scope creep by month two.

## When to raise the rate

At the end of the stated trial period, if Belmont wants to continue: that's the point to renegotiate toward a real market rate for the actual workload being done — not immediately to the $1,500-3,000 full-project range, but a genuine step up from the intro rate, justified by the trial period's delivered work rather than by time having simply passed.

## If Belmont says no

Still worth asking for permission to reference the mockup publicly as a portfolio case study, even without a paid outcome — that's useful for any future conversation, including eventually with one of the other five companies, without having built anything company-specific for them yet.
